# Citation verdicts

Checked against pages opened for this note. Verdicts are only `verified`, `unverified`, or `misattributed`. No page numbers or quotations are invented.

1. **Bengt Holmström (2015), “Understanding the Role of Debt in the Financial System” (BIS Working Paper 479).** Verdict: **verified.** The paper argues that money-market debt is information-insensitive and that, unlike stock markets, money markets work by obviating price discovery with over-collateralized debt. URL: https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-479-understanding-role-debt-financial-system.pdf

2. **Gary Gorton (2010), *Slapped by the Invisible Hand: The Panic of 2007*.** Verdict: **verified.** Gorton treats a banking panic as the breakdown of debt that was designed to be information-insensitive: once that debt can no longer be traded without due diligence, quantities (haircuts, renewals) adjust and a run follows. The same regime switch is stated in his joint work with Dang and Holmström. URL: https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780199742110_A23609137/preview-9780199742110_A23609137.pdf

3. **Tri Vi Dang, Gary Gorton, and Bengt Holmström, “Ignorance, Debt, and Financial Crises” (working paper; related statement in their 2020 *Annual Review of Financial Economics* survey).** Verdict: **verified.** They argue that debt is the least information-sensitive contract, that symmetric ignorance supports liquidity, and that a crisis is the shift in which information-insensitive debt becomes information-sensitive. URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26074/w26074.pdf

4. **Thomas J. Sargent and Neil Wallace (1982), “The Real-Bills Doctrine versus the Quantity Theory: A Reconsideration,” *Journal of Political Economy*.** Verdict: **verified.** The paper sets the real-bills prescription (unfettered intermediation) against the quantity-theory prescription (restrictions that separate money from credit) and finds the real-bills regime Pareto optimal in their overlapping-generations examples even when prices fluctuate more. URL: https://www.minneapolisfed.org/research/staff-reports/the-real-bills-doctrine-vs-the-quantity-theory-a-reconsideration

5. **Lloyd W. Mints (1945), *A History of Banking Theory in Great Britain and the United States*.** Verdict: **verified.** Mints coined “real-bills doctrine” in this history of banking theory and made a critique of that doctrine the book’s central theme: restricting banks to bona fide commercial loans does not automatically give the right quantity of money. URL: https://www.bankofgreece.gr/Publications/Paper2019264.pdf

6. **John Fullarton (1844), *On the Regulation of Currencies*.** Verdict: **verified.** Fullarton, on the Banking School side, attacked the Currency School’s case for quantitative limits on note issue and aligned the “law of reflux” with the real-bills doctrine: notes issued against real transactions return to the banks and cannot be kept in permanent excess. URL: https://www.hetwebsite.net/het/profiles/fullarton.htm

7. **Henry Thornton (1802), *An Enquiry into the Nature and Effects of the Paper Credit of Great Britain*.** Verdict: **verified.** *Paper Credit* is the classical monetary treatise it is said to be, and Thornton is a standard critic of the real-bills view: discounting “real” bills does not by itself limit the note issue, because the demand for paper can expand with prices. URL: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/meltzer/hethen87.pdf

8. **Nobuhiro Kiyotaki and Randall Wright (1989), “On Money as a Medium of Exchange,” *Journal of Political Economy*.** Verdict: **verified.** This is a search model in which agents meet bilaterally and some goods, or fiat currency, are held only to be traded later, so the reason anyone holds money is that it relaxes the double coincidence of wants. URL: https://journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/261634

9. **Ricardo Lagos and Randall Wright (2005), “A Unified Framework for Monetary Theory and Policy Analysis,” *Journal of Political Economy*.** Verdict: **verified.** The paper is a monetary-search model whose point is the frictions that make money essential (anonymous meetings, no enforceable future promises) while remaining usable for policy analysis. URL: https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/27329/2/S-MF-03-21.pdf

10. **Narayana R. Kocherlakota (1998), “Money Is Memory,” *Journal of Economic Theory*.** Verdict: **verified.** The paper’s main proposition is that any allocation feasible with money is feasible with memory of trading histories, so money is technologically a primitive form of memory. URL: https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uwm.edu/dist/8/268/files/2019/01/Kocherlakota-1998-JET-11a7m8w.pdf

11. **Ben Bernanke, Mark Gertler, and Simon Gilchrist (1996), “The Financial Accelerator and the Flight to Quality,” *Review of Economics and Statistics* (NBER Working Paper 4789, 1994).** Verdict: **verified.** They define the financial accelerator as the amplification of shocks by endogenous changes in the agency costs of lending, with a flight to quality away from high-agency-cost borrowers in downturns. URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w4789

12. **Markus K. Brunnermeier and Lasse Heje Pedersen (2009), “Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity,” *Review of Financial Studies*.** Verdict: **verified.** The model links market liquidity to funding liquidity and names a margin spiral: price moves raise margins, which tighten funding and further reduce market liquidity. URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12939/w12939.pdf

13. **Nobuhiro Kiyotaki and John Moore (1997), “Credit Cycles,” *Journal of Political Economy*.** Verdict: **verified.** Durable assets are both inputs and collateral, so credit limits and asset prices feed on each other and small temporary shocks produce large, persistent fluctuations. URL: https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/lchrist/d11/d1118/kiyotaki-moore.pdf

14. **Hyman P. Minsky, “The Financial Instability Hypothesis” (Levy Economics Institute Working Paper No. 74; developed in *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy*, 1986).** Verdict: **verified.** Minsky argues that prolonged prosperity shifts financing from hedge toward speculative and Ponzi units, so capitalist economies generate their own instability rather than merely reacting to outside shocks. URL: https://www.levyinstitute.org/pubs/wp74.pdf

15. **Tobias Adrian and Hyun Song Shin (2010), “Liquidity and Leverage,” *Journal of Financial Intermediation* (FRBNY Staff Report 328).** Verdict: **verified.** They document that marked-to-market intermediary leverage is strongly procyclical: balance sheets expand in booms and contract in busts, with repos as the main margin of adjustment. URL: https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr328.pdf

16. **Charles A. E. Goodhart (1975), “Problems of Monetary Management: The U.K. Experience,” in *Papers in Monetary Economics* (Reserve Bank of Australia); restated in *Monetary Theory and Practice* (1984).** Verdict: **verified.** Goodhart’s own statement is that any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes. The parenthetical slogan (a measure ceases to be good once it is a target) is Marilyn Strathern’s 1997 paraphrase of that law, not a different claim. URL: https://lelibellio.polytechnique.fr/uploads/volume9_num4/articles/goodharts_law.pdf

17. **Robert E. Lucas Jr. (1976), “Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique,” *Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy*.** Verdict: **verified.** The paper argues that policy evaluation with fixed econometric relationships is invalid because those relationships embed expectations of the policy regime and change when the regime changes. URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/crcspp/v1y1976ip19-46.html

18. **Robin Hanson, “Logarithmic Market Scoring Rules for Modular Combinatorial Information Aggregation” (*Journal of Prediction Markets*, 2007; earlier as “Combinatorial Information Market Design”).** Verdict: **verified.** Hanson defines the logarithmic market scoring rule and shows that, among market scoring rules, the logarithmic version preserves conditional probabilities and conditional independence. URL: https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/mktscore.pdf

19. **Robin Hanson and Ryan Oprea (2009), “A Manipulator Can Aid Prediction Market Accuracy,” *Economica*.** Verdict: **verified.** In their Kyle-style model, uncertainty about a manipulator’s target price raises the return to becoming informed, so a larger variance of the manipulator’s target can increase average price accuracy. URL: https://ryanoprea.com/papers/manipulator-can-aid-prediction-markets.pdf

20. **Justin Wolfers and Eric Zitzewitz (2006), “Interpreting Prediction Market Prices as Probabilities” (NBER Working Paper 12200).** Verdict: **verified.** They give conditions under which a binary prediction-market price equals traders’ mean belief, and argue that prices are usually close to that mean even when the equality is not exact. URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12200/w12200.pdf

21. **Marco Ottaviani and Peter Norman Sørensen, “Aggregation of Information and Beliefs: Asset Pricing Lessons from Prediction Markets” (University of Copenhagen discussion paper; related 2010 *AEJ: Micro* paper on the favorite-longshot bias).** Verdict: **verified.** With heterogeneous priors and limited budgets, the rational-expectations price underreacts to information, so the market price is not the Bayesian aggregate; they identify this with the favorite-longshot bias. URL: https://web.econ.ku.dk/sorensen/papers/AggregationofInformationandBeliefs.pdf

22. **Simon Gilchrist and Egon Zakrajšek (2012), “Credit Spreads and Business Cycle Fluctuations,” *American Economic Review*.** Verdict: **verified.** They decompose a corporate credit-spread index into expected-default risk and a residual, the excess bond premium, defined as compensation for credit risk beyond expected defaults. URL: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.102.4.1692

23. **Francis A. Longstaff, Sanjay Mithal, and Eric Neis (2005), “Corporate Yield Spreads: Default Risk or Liquidity? New Evidence from the Credit Default Swap Market,” *Journal of Finance*.** Verdict: **verified.** Using CDS premia, they measure a default component and a nondefault component of corporate spreads. They find that default risk is the majority of the spread in every rating category they study, and also that it is not the whole spread: a significant nondefault component, tied to illiquidity, remains (about 20–100 basis points against Treasuries in their sample). That is the opposite emphasis from papers that find expected default explains only a small slice of investment-grade spreads. URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w10418

24. **J. L. Kelly Jr. (1956), “A New Interpretation of Information Rate,” *Bell System Technical Journal*.** Verdict: **verified.** Kelly shows that a gambler who reinvests and bets in proportion to the probabilities implied by a channel maximizes the exponential growth rate of capital, and that this maximum growth rate equals the information rate of the channel when the odds are fair. URL: http://stat.wharton.upenn.edu/~steele/Resources/FTSResources/KellyBreiman/Kelly56.pdf

25. **Thomas M. Cover and Joy A. Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, chapter 6, “Gambling and Data Compression” (2nd ed., 2006; same chapter in the 1991 edition).** Verdict: **verified.** Chapter 6 defines the doubling rate, shows that the growth-rate gap between the true distribution and a wrong betting distribution is the relative entropy \(D(p\|q)\) (their equation writes the doubling rate as a difference of relative entropies), and proves that the increase in doubling rate from side information equals the mutual information \(I(X;Y)\). URL: https://ftp.esat.kuleuven.be/pub/SISTA/decock/voor_xander/referenties/Cover&Thomas/6.pdf

26. **Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971), *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process* (Harvard University Press).** Verdict: **verified.** The book’s stated theme is that the economic process is entropic rather than a mechanical analogue: economic scarcity reflects the entropy law because activity uses low-entropy resources. URL: https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~lozada/Adv_Resource_Econ/En_Law_Econ_Proc_Cropped_Optimized_Clearscan.pdf

27. **Robert U. Ayres and Benjamin Warr (2005), “Accounting for Growth: The Role of Physical Work,” *Structural Change and Economic Dynamics*.** Verdict: **verified.** They argue that U.S. growth is accounted for by exergy converted into useful physical work, together with capital and labor, rather than by raw energy plus an exogenous Solow residual. URL: https://dyrehaugen.github.io/recs/pdf/Ayres_2005_Accounting_for_Growth_Physical_Work.pdf

28. **Rolf Landauer (1961), “Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process,” *IBM Journal of Research and Development*.** Verdict: **verified.** Landauer argues that logically irreversible operations have a minimum heat cost, of order \(kT\) per irreversible function (the bound later stated as \(kT \ln 2\) per erased bit). URL: https://www.dna.caltech.edu/courses/cs191/paperscs191/landauer1961.pdf

29. **John Maynard Keynes, International Clearing Union / bancor proposals (1941–43).** Verdict: **misattributed.** Keynes did propose an International Clearing Union whose unit, bancor, would settle international balances, and he separately discussed commodity buffer stocks financed through that union. Bancor in the plan is international bank money fixed, but not unalterably, in terms of gold. It is not a commodity-basket standard. A currency backed by a basket of raw materials is Benjamin Graham’s commodity-reserve proposal, which Keynes did not adopt as the definition of bancor. URL: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781451972511/ch001.xml

30. **F. A. Hayek (1976), *Denationalisation of Money* (Institute of Economic Affairs; refined edition 1978).** Verdict: **verified.** Hayek argues for abolishing the government monopoly of money and allowing privately issued currencies to compete, with users selecting the issuers who best preserve value. URL: https://iea.org.uk/publications/research/denationalisation-of-money-0/

31. **David Graeber (2011), *Debt: The First 5,000 Years*.** Verdict: **verified.** Graeber argues that the textbook sequence barter-then-money-then-credit is backwards: credit and units of account for recording obligations precede coinage, and early money functions as an abstract ledger of debts. His own restatement of the book’s thesis is what was opened. URL: https://davidgraeber.org/articles/to-have-is-to-owe/

32. **Henry Ford, interview in the *New-York Tribune*, 4 December 1921 (“Ford Would Replace Gold with Energy Currency and Stop Wars”).** Verdict: **verified.** The remark exists. Ford, discussing Muscle Shoals, said the standard could be a certain amount of energy exerted for one hour equal to one dollar, in place of the gold dollar, and that the government should issue currency against that power rather than interest-bearing bonds. URL: https://21ideas.org/en/energy-backed-money/ (transcription of the 4 December 1921 *New-York Tribune* page; the Library of Congress OCR of that page, https://chroniclingamerica.loc.gov/lccn/sn83030214/1921-12-04/ed-1/seq-6/, contains the same “energy exerted for one hour” wording)
